Proofs follow the source:
- Completion within a busy interval: the closing quiet time.
- A pending higher-or-equal-priority job between a quiet and a non-quiet instant.
- Idle instants: work-bearing readiness and work conservation give a quiet next instant.
- A pending higher-or-equal-priority job at every instant of a busy prefix, and hence no idle instant.
- Jobs released before a quiet time: they are completed and so receive no further service. The filtered sum splits over `arrivals_between_cat`.
- No idle time without quiet times: in a unit-service, ideal-progress uniprocessor, the total service at each instant is exactly one.
- Busy-prefix existence: the greatest quiet time `≤ t_busy` (`Nat.findGreatest`).
- Uninterrupted service: the interval length equals higher-or-equal-priority service plus lower-priority service. The lower-priority service is bounded pointwise by the priority-inversion indicator, and cumulatively by the bound. The bound is taken directly, or via `cumulative_priority_inversion_cat` when the interval is shorter than the prefix.
- Too much workload: a pending higher-or-equal-priority job has received strictly less than its cost.
- Boundedness: the least quiet time in `(t1, t1 + Δ]` (`Nat.find`), or otherwise the contradiction `Δ < Δ`.
- Existence and the response-time bound: the job is pending at its arrival, and completion is monotone.

Lean axioms: only the standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. The build contains no `sorry`.
