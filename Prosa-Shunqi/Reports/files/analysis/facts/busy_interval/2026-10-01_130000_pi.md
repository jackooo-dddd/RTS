Proofs follow the source:
- **Lower-priority job scheduled at `t` in a prefix:** no instant of `[t1, t]` is a preemption time. At such an instant its segment would restart (`scheduling_of_any_segment_starts_with_preemption_time_continuously_sched`), and the accepted `hep_at_pt` lemma makes it higher-or-equal-priority. Hence:
  - it is scheduled continuously back to `t1` (`neg_pt_scheduled_continuously_after`);
  - it arrived before `t1`;
  - it was scheduled at `t1 - 1` (`neg_pt_scheduled_at`).
- **Single priority-inversion job:**
  - The witness is extracted from the Boolean priority-inversion definition.
  - Continuity comes from the above.
  - Uniqueness comes from the uniprocessor property.
  - The case split rests on continuity from `t1`.
- **Maximum task segment bound:** pointwise monotonicity of `bigMaxListCond` over the job-level `job_respects_max_nonpreemptive_segment`.
- **Preemption-time existence**, by cases on `t1`:
  - idle: `idle_time_is_pt`;
  - higher-or-equal priority scheduled: `zero_is_pt` / `first_moment_is_pt`;
  - lower priority scheduled: the least preemption point after the job's current progress (`Nat.find`). The job runs continuously until it, since with unit service and ideal progress its service equals elapsed time. It is bounded by the maximum lower-priority nonpreemptive segment, because the job arrived before `t1` and has positive remaining cost.
- **After a preemption point:** a lower-priority job's segment would start at a preemption time inside the prefix, which is impossible. The cumulative bound splits the interval sum at the preemption point (`Finset.sum_Ico_consecutive`).
